Bayesian models for day-to-day emotion dynamics

STAT 405 Bayesian Statistics, UBCApr 2026two-person project

Self-reported emotions are coarse, noisy snapshots of an underlying state. Which Bayesian model best describes how happiness evolves over a day, and is fast variational inference a safe substitute for Hamiltonian Monte Carlo?

Data

Experience-sampling data from Kullar et al. (2024): 105 participants rating nine emotions on a 1–7 scale, five prompts a day.

Approach

  • An ordered-logistic measurement model linking each 7-point rating to a continuous latent state.
  • Three nested latent processes: independent states; autoregression on the previous prompt and the same prompt the day before (lag 5); and that model plus lagged enthusiastic, pleased and relaxed.
  • Focal emotion and predictors chosen by leave-one-subject-out, one-step-ahead screening.
  • Every model fit in Stan with NUTS and with mean-field VI, compared by PSIS-LOO, and checked with R̂, bulk and tail ESS, rank plots and exact invariance tests.

Adding the latent time structure is the large gain: expected log predictive density rises by 1,893 under HMC. Lagged emotions add a further 38.

HMC beats mean-field VI for every model, and the gap is widest once temporal dependence enters (471 for the AR model), consistent with VI missing posterior correlation.

I led the modeling: I developed the three models, implemented them in Stan, ran the diagnostics and the model comparison, and compiled the results.

What actually helps a small CNN generalize?

Deep Learning & AI, Peking UniversityOct 2026solo

For a convolutional network trained from scratch on Dogs vs. Cats, how much do batch normalization and data augmentation each contribute, and do their benefits stack?

Setup

  • A 2 × 2 ablation: BatchNorm on or off, crossed with augmentation (padded random crop and horizontal flip) on or off.
  • The same 319,874-parameter CNN in every arm (three convolutional blocks, Kaiming initialization), with the same seed, batch order and optimizer (Adam, learning rate 10⁻³) for 30 epochs.
  • 15,000 training and 10,000 validation images. Parameter counts checked by hand against the model summary.

Augmentation is the largest effect: +7.3 points of final validation accuracy (80.3% to 87.6%), and it removed the late-epoch decline the baseline showed. BatchNorm adds +2.2, mostly by steadying late training.

The two did not stack: with both on, accuracy peaked at 86.4% and ended at 82.5%. With one seed per arm, that interaction needs repeated runs before it means anything.

A leakage-free model comparison for brand choice

Machine Learning & AI, Peking UniversityOct 2026solo

Predict whether a shopper buys Citrus Hill or Minute Maid orange juice from 1,070 purchases (the ISLP OJ data), using an evaluation protocol that cannot leak information from the test set.

Approach

  • scikit-learn pipelines, so encoding and scaling are fit inside each fold, with one fixed set of five stratified folds for every model.
  • Grid-searched logistic regression, k-nearest neighbors, a decision tree and gradient boosting, and traced their bias–variance curves.
  • Found that nine of the 17 predictors duplicate information in others (store recodings and derived price columns), and showed that a compact eight-predictor set loses nothing.
  • Scored the chosen model once on the untouched test set, then stress-tested it by training on early weeks and predicting later ones.

The compact logistic regression reached 84.1% test accuracy and 0.92 ROC-AUC, against a 61% majority baseline. On future weeks it held at 80.3% and 0.89.

Brand loyalty dominates both the coefficients and permutation importance, followed by the price gap between brands.

Deep learning from first principles

A sequence of PyTorch builds. On simulated regression data (n = 1,000, p = 10, 100 replications) I compared closed-form OLS with full-batch and mini-batch gradient descent, studied how learning rate and batch count shape convergence, checked autograd against hand-derived gradients, and tested Adam. I then fit a two-hidden-layer MLP for image regression and a LeNet-style CNN for image classification on a 5,500-image face dataset.

Mini-batch GD matched OLS to four decimals in RMSE; the CNN reached 93.6% validation accuracy vs 84.2% for logistic regression on the same pixels
